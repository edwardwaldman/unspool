# Photosynthesis (Biology, Unit 3)

Photosynthesis is how plants, algae and some bacteria turn light energy into chemical energy stored in sugar.
It happens in the chloroplasts, mostly in the leaf's mesophyll cells. Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll, a green
pigment that absorbs red and blue light and reflects green light, which is why leaves look green.

Overall equation: 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6 O2.

## Stage 1: the light-dependent reactions
- Take place in the thylakoid membranes.
- Light splits water (photolysis), releasing oxygen as a by-product.
- The energy is stored in ATP and NADPH.

## Stage 2: the Calvin cycle (light-independent reactions)
- Takes place in the stroma.
- Uses ATP and NADPH from stage 1 to fix carbon dioxide into sugar.
- The enzyme RuBisCO fixes CO2 to a 5-carbon sugar called RuBP.

## Factors that limit the rate
Light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature. Past a point, raising one factor no longer
speeds things up because another factor becomes the limit.

## Common exam traps
- The oxygen released comes from water, not from carbon dioxide.
- The Calvin cycle does not need light directly, but it stops in the dark because it runs out of ATP and NADPH.
