# The French Revolution (1789–1799)

Causes: France was nearly bankrupt after costly wars, including support for the American Revolution. Taxes fell
mostly on the Third Estate (commoners), while the clergy and nobility paid little. Poor harvests in 1788 made bread
expensive. Enlightenment ideas about rights and the consent of the governed spread.

1789: Louis XVI called the Estates-General to raise money. The Third Estate broke away and declared itself the
National Assembly, swearing the Tennis Court Oath not to separate until France had a constitution. On 14 July 1789
crowds stormed the Bastille, a royal prison and fortress. In August the Assembly issued the Declaration of the
Rights of Man and of the Citizen.

1792: France became a republic. 1793: Louis XVI was executed. The Reign of Terror (1793–1794), led by the Committee
of Public Safety and Maximilien Robespierre, executed thousands of people seen as enemies of the revolution.
Robespierre himself was executed in July 1794.

1799: Napoleon Bonaparte seized power in a coup, ending the revolutionary decade.
