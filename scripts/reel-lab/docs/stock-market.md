# How the stock market works

A share (stock) is a small piece of ownership in a company. Companies sell shares to raise money, first through
an initial public offering (IPO). After that, investors buy and sell shares with each other on an exchange such as
the New York Stock Exchange or Nasdaq.

Prices move with supply and demand. When more people want to buy a stock than sell it, the price rises; when more
want to sell, it falls. Expectations about a company's future profits drive that demand.

Shareholders can earn money two ways: the share price rising (a capital gain) and dividends, a share of profits
some companies pay out.

An index tracks a group of stocks. The S&P 500 tracks 500 large US companies; the Dow Jones Industrial Average
tracks 30. An index fund buys every stock in an index, so investors spread their risk instead of betting on one
company.

Risk: share prices can fall as well as rise, and a company can go bankrupt, making its shares worthless.
Diversification (owning many different investments) reduces the risk of one company's failure.
